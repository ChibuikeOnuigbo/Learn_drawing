# Verified production lessons

## Speech and reaction timing

Use actual word events when synthesizing test speech. Word-count estimates drift because sentence lengths differ. Place the visual on the semantic punchline, and preserve evidence of the selected word, timestamp and output frame.

Whisper tiny misheard the longer demo's conclusion. Base.en recovered all three reaction phrases in that example, but word starts still differed from synthesis events by up to 0.279 seconds. Short speech sometimes lost the plural s in “brains”; semantic matching now accepts the singular variant and keeps a real audio-timing regression. This is not proof of exact ASR or general human-speech accuracy. Low confidence and ASR-based sync remain flagged for review.

A literal medical/scientific brain reference should abstain rather than use a stupidity gag. The classifier's raw fixed holdout score is 19/20; explicit negation handling is an editorial guard, not learned language understanding. Three bundled asset families are a small library, not a universal best-meme oracle.

## Video decoding and reference study

Pip OpenCV could not decode the supplied AV1 references. The installed FFmpeg software decoder could; the study script now uses FFmpeg. All 55 currently visible videos yielded six samples. A 184-entry catalog was not evidence of 184 available uploads. No file was obtained from YouTube directly in this iteration.

CLIP searches sampled frames and can misrank stylistic queries. It does not recover animation rigs, brush paths or a creator's production process. The video files were not used to fine-tune CLIP or a generative drawing model.

## 2D and editing

Nested groups keep eyes, hair, hands and other parts attached during pose changes. Independent X/Y scaling enables blinks without shrinking the entire eye uniformly. Parent visibility must propagate to children; the renderer has an image-level regression for this.

Equal-quarter acting beats did not align with narration sentences. The character exercise retimes those beats to speech events. A clean visual master prevents the explainer from showing two sets of burned captions. Explicit final-frame holds let a shot remain readable through a longer narration segment without silently truncating audio.

## 3D rigging

The humanoid uses 15 actual bones, 5,994 weighted mesh vertices and four IK chains. Foot orientation needs its own control so shin rotations do not point the feet into the floor. The measured floor minima and attachment errors are in each rig report. The prop follows the evaluated hand after grasp; this is baked procedural animation, not a learned manipulation policy or physics simulation.

The cloud Blender build lacks OpenImageDenoise support. A denoising test failed; supported CPU presets explicitly disable it. Grain remains visible in low-sample preview renders. Do not claim a missing denoiser ran successfully.

## Learning scope

Repeated renders and these notes improve reproducible workflows, not neural weights. The new neural/linear training task in this iteration is small supervised narration-intent classification. General drawing-policy training, advanced character animation and reinforcement learning remain separate research work requiring appropriate data, models and evaluation.
